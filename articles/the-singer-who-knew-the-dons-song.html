The Singer Who Knew the Don's Song

2026-11-16

By Adrian Cole Stories — the full story. The reels show only the beginning; the ending lives here.

It was a Thursday night at the Marquee, the Don's own club, when the band stopped playing and a young singer took the stage.

She wore a black evening dress, and around her neck hung a silver necklace with a small locket. She looked out at the room, at the men in their suits and the women in their diamonds, and she began to sing.

Don Adrian Cole froze.

He had not heard that song in thirty years. His wife had written it, long ago, on a piano in this very building — and she had never sung it for anyone. Not for a crowd. Not for a recording. It was hers alone, and when she died, the song had died with her.

"Nobody sings that song," the Don said, rising from his table. His voice cut through the room, and the singer stopped. "My wife wrote it — and she never sang it for anyone."

The singer looked down at him from the stage. She was twenty-six years old, with long dark wavy hair, and she was not afraid.

"She sang it for me," she said. "Every night, before bed."

The room went silent. The Don walked slowly toward the stage, his men falling back behind him.

"Who are you?" he asked.

The singer held up the silver necklace. It caught the light, and the Don's face went pale. He knew that necklace. He had given it to his wife himself, on their wedding day.

"My wife died thirty years ago," he said. "She left nothing."

"Then why," the singer asked, "does this necklace have your name inside it?"
